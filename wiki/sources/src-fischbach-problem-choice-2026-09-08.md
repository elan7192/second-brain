Durable METHOD for **problem picking** (Fischbach / Walsh lineage via Stanford Eng 10-tip digest of the Cell framework): spend disproportionate time on choice vs execution; structure ideation; then navigate the decision tree.

### 10-point skeleton (Stanford Eng article)

1. **Spend more time** — months-scale brainstorm practice; problem choice is a skill like yoga, not a rush.
2. **Use structure as a resource** — put something concrete on paper (haiku-like constraints beat blank page).
3. **Spark inspiration** — “intuition pumps” (Dennett): optimize current process; path from now→future; observe what works/doesn’t.
4. **Avoid the traps** — easy-only; over-narrow specialty; late trend-joining; blaming externals; copying process theater without building the process. Conversation spots missed traps.
5. **Get comfortable with risk** — nothing worth doing is sure to succeed; name risks honestly to get others aboard.
6. **Go for the goal** — articulate motivation / what you’re solving for (niche-ok if honest).
7. **Keep the details flexible** — fix the goal; don’t force-combine interests (e.g. AI+climate) if the tool doesn’t fit the problem.
8. **Expect twists and turns** — plan = ensemble of paths; get knocked off one → switch.
9. **Altitude dance** — ~90% doing / ~10% zoom-out update; cycle immersion ↔ critical evaluation (Cell: cannot do both at once).
10. **Face problems head on** — adversity has high information content; nonresistance / use the clue (martial-arts metaphor).

Cell abstract framing (verified): ideation prompts; evaluate impact × likelihood of success; **fix one parameter at a time**; opportunities from failure. Days choosing / years solving imbalance limits impact.
